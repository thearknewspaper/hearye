Getting started

HearYe saves the audio of public meetings — city councils, school boards, commissions — as .m4a files ready for a transcription app. It does two jobs:

  1. Download one meeting from a link you paste.
  2. Watch your beat's channels and archives, and fetch each new meeting on its own, even with the window closed.

Everything it needs (yt-dlp, FFmpeg and a JavaScript runtime) is built in. There's nothing else to install.

Downloading one meeting

  1. Paste, type or drag a link into the box: a YouTube video, a Granicus meeting page, Vimeo, Zoom recording, or a direct audio/video file.
  2. HearYe inspects it automatically and shows the title it found.
  3. Optionally rename the file, pick a format, then click Download audio (or press Return).

A long meeting takes a minute or two to convert after downloading. The file appears in your save folder only once it's finished, so a transcription app watching that folder never grabs half a file.

Watching for new meetings

  1. In Watch for new meetings, paste a source (see the next section) and click Add.
  2. Choose how often to check: every hour, 6 hours, 12 hours or day.
  3. Turn on Watch my selected channels.

From then on, HearYe checks in the background, even after you quit it and after a restart. When a meeting appears it downloads the audio and sends an alert that stays until you dismiss it. Click Allow the first time macOS asks about notifications.

  • Only new meetings: recordings already posted when you start watching are left alone, not downloaded as a backlog.
  • Live streams are fetched once the recording is finished.
  • Check now checks immediately, whether watching is on or not.

Not working for your city's system (Swagit, BoardDocs, Legistar, CivicPlus video)? Paste a single meeting link into the download box first. If that works, use Help ▸ Report a Problem… to ask for that system to be supported for watching.

Where audio goes

By default, ~/Downloads. Click Change… beside "Saved to" to pick any folder; downloads and watched meetings both use it. Point a transcription app's watch folder (MacWhisper, for example) at the same place.

Choose App… opens each new file in an app of your choice as soon as it's saved.

Files keep the source's audio quality and channels. A 69-minute meeting is about 125 MB.

Download engine

YouTube changes its site often, and yt-dlp, the engine HearYe uses, changes to keep up. HearYe checks for a new yt-dlp once a day and installs it after verifying its published checksum. HearYe ▸ Download Engine… shows the version in use, lets you update now, or go back to the version that came with HearYe.

Common problems

"Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" or a YouTube download fails

Open HearYe ▸ Download Engine… and click Update yt-dlp, then try again. If it keeps failing, YouTube may be rate-limiting your network. Wait an hour.

No alert when a meeting arrives

Check System Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ HearYe. On a Mac managed by an IT department, notifications may be blocked; HearYe then shows a dialog with its icon instead.

Watching stopped after I moved or updated the app

Open HearYe once. It repairs the background job whenever the app's location changes.

A source was added but nothing downloads

Only meetings posted after you started watching are fetched. Use Check now after the next meeting goes up, or paste an older meeting into the download box.

Something else

Help ▸ Report a Problem… gathers the version and recent log for you to review before anything is sent.

FAQ

Is downloading meetings allowed?

HearYe is meant for public meetings that governments post for the public. Use only media you're allowed to download, and follow each provider's terms.

Does HearYe send my data anywhere?

No. It talks only to the sites you point it at, to GitHub once a day to check for updates to HearYe and yt-dlp, and to nothing else.

Is it free?

Yes. HearYe was built for The Ark, the weekly newspaper serving Tiburon, Belvedere and Strawberry since 1973, and is free for any newsroom under the MIT License. It bundles yt-dlp, FFmpeg, Python and deno under their own licenses; see About HearYe ▸ Acknowledgments.
